Library layout (rail + browser column)

each state has a hash, read on load, e.g. #library, #library-1100

The layout in one line

In Library the app's sidebar folds into a 50 px rail of page icons, and the space goes to a browser column (Favourites, playlists, folders, a filter field) that the user drags wider or narrower. The right panel leaves Library: its summary card becomes a strip on the page, and the track's details become an inspector that opens only when asked. Every other page keeps the full sidebar and its docked panel. The user can fold the sidebar on any page with ⌃⌘S.

Decisions

  1. One sidebar, two forms: full (168 px) and rail (50 px). The rail is the full sidebar with its labels folded away. The glyph column (18–32 px from the window's edge) and each row's height (30 px, 2 px apart) are the same in both forms, so no icon moves when the sidebar folds. The selected pill becomes a 30 × 30 square on the same spot. The fold button sits on that glyph column too, at the top of both forms (the macOS sidebar symbol, "Hide sidebar ⌃⌘S" / "Show sidebar ⌃⌘S"), so it never moves either, and the word "Discjockey" folds away beside it. Why: navigation stays obvious when a DJ's eye finds To-do at the same place in either form. Opening a collection scrolls its row into view in the browser. Every rail icon has a tooltip with its name and shortcut, and the first one waits 500 ms, then the next ones open at once (the InfoTip rule).
  2. Library gets a nav item of its own (♫, after Download limits), and its collections leave the app sidebar. The full sidebar holds only the pages, plus Favourites as shortcuts (no counts) on pages where the browser column isn't shown. A favourite opens Library at that playlist or folder. Why: the A variant's "everything in the sidebar" was what made names cut and the sidebar long. Favourites are short, the user picks them, and they stay one click away from To-do or Sync, which was A's real advantage. Without counts, 168 px fits "Bounce · Old Sets".
  3. The browser column is chrome, not page. It uses the frame's material beside the rail, so the L frame just grows wider in Library (50 + 240 px). The page keeps its single edge line, and a quiet hairline (the same 6 % white) separates the rail from the browser, marking the two levels: pages | collections. The filter field sits at the top and stays visible ("Filter playlists, folders ⌘K", centred on the page header's row). The track search stays top right on the page ("Search title, artist… ⌘F"). The two fields use different words, sit in different materials and have different shortcuts. Why visible, not behind a magnifier: the column has the room, and a new user should see that they can filter.
  4. Resizable by drag, 200–360 px, 240 by default. The grip is the page's edge line (9 px wide). On hover the pointer turns into the resize cursor, and while dragging the line turns orange (the action) and follows the pointer 1:1, with no animation. Double-clicking the line returns to 240. The width is remembered. Every row is now one line: "Rekordbox Library", "Bounce · Old Sets" and "Hardtechno › 2019" (the same words as the page's Subfolder column) all fit at 240 px. The two-line crate rows and the balanced wrap for watched folders are gone. A playlist name that's still too long ("Afterhour_Sonntag_Warmup_…") ends in "…" with a tooltip, and comes out whole when the column is dragged wider.
  5. The right panel leaves Library, because the width doesn't add up. With the rail, the browser and a docked 360 px panel, the 1280 table would be 586 px, less than today's 708. So: the summary card becomes a 72 px strip between the header and the filter segments. It keeps the share, the bar, Upgrade ready and Not lossless yet · n in To-do, each with its count and link. The bar's legend is dropped, because the segments right below it carry the same dots and counts. The track details become an inspector (the same card content, 360 / 320 px): it opens from the header's details toggle, ⌘I, the right-click menu's "Show details", or a click on a row's quality badge ("Why is this weak?"). It then follows the selection and stays open until closed. Its state is remembered, and it starts closed. A click selects and a double-click or Enter plays, as today. Why the badge: the quality question is what makes a DJ want the details, and the badge is where they ask it.
  6. The list stays fully usable at 1280 × 800 and at 1100 × 700; its columns follow its own width (the container rule, nothing scrolls sideways). The drop order is Length first, then Playlist, then Subfolder (D10). What I chose: with details closed, which is the normal state, Library at 1280 shows more than today (+ Length), and at 1100 it shows what 1280 shows today. With details open, the list never goes below today's 1100 set (Track, Artist, Subfolder, Quality, about 586 px), and the facts of the columns it drops are in the inspector for the selected track: Playlists in its file card, the place in its head ("in Hardtechno › 2019"). The filter segments keep their whole labels in every state. Variant B's failure (a list too narrow to be useful, single-letter filters) can't happen.
  7. Below 1240 px the window holds the browser or the details beside the list, not both. At 1100, opening details steps the browser column back behind the rail (the rail stays, with Library's pill, and ⌘K or a click on ♫ brings the browser back and closes the details). Closing details brings the browser back at its own width. Why: rail + browser + a 320 px inspector leave 446 px at 1100, which is variant B's narrowness. Choosing a collection and reading one track's details are different moments, so on a small screen they take turns. At 1280 and up both fit.
  8. Sidebar memory: two places, not per page. Library remembers its form (rail by default) and all other pages share one form (full by default). Changed from the brief ("remembered per page"): per page, folding the sidebar on Dashboard and then opening Sync would unfold it again, which feels like the app ignoring you, and every page switch could animate the chrome. With two places the chrome changes only on the way into or out of Library, and that change is expected because Library has its own browser.
  9. ⌘K works on every page: it opens Library with the filter focused (from any page, any collection is ⌘K + a few letters + Enter). The filter behaves as decided for A: it filters names, keeps sections and the watched folder as context, opens folded sections, finds hidden crates and the 312 albums, and puts the keyboard highlight on the first match.
  10. Discoverable without being told: the fold button with its tooltip; visible chevrons on every section header and watched folder; section headers that stick to the list's edges, so "Folders ⌄" sits at the foot from the first look; an empty Favourites section that shows one dashed row "Drag a playlist or folder here" until the first pin (it folds and stays folded if the user wants); the right-click menu (Open · Play · Add to favourites · Hide from sidebar · Show in Finder) with the note "Settings brings it back"; and the resize cursor plus the line lighting up on the column's edge.
  11. The rail carries the sidebar's signals, in the same colours: the To-do count as a neutral pill on the glyph's corner; Download limits' yellow dot where it is today; a running sync as a neutral ring round ⟳ (the arc is the share done; in the full form, "12/40" in the count column). The hidden player becomes ⌃ Show player above a play button whose orange ring is the position, the same orange as the foot's line.
  12. The foot doesn't change. It stays full window width under the rail and the browser, with the transport centred on the window. Its line (the page's lower edge and the position) starts at the page's edge, so in Library it starts at 290 px: the line follows --sidebar-w, which becomes the chrome's whole width.
  13. Folder logic as decided: only crates are listed (wrappers skipped, full path in the tooltip), a crate can be hidden (right-click, Settings → Watched folders), and above 25 crates the watched folder is one row with Settings' message and the page link "Show some in the sidebar…" ("sidebar" here means the Library list, which is what the browser column is). The offline "!", the dimmed switched-off playlist, Favourites by drag, and Playlists in priority order or A–Z are all unchanged.

Motion: the page lays out once, the chrome moves over it

Reflowing a 500-row table on every frame would stutter, and it would make the columns flicker. So the page takes its final width at once, and only the chrome moves: when the chrome shrinks it uncovers the page, and when it grows it covers space that is already empty. This is the foot's rule ("the page makes room at once, only the contents move"), applied to the sidebar and the inspector. Only transform, opacity and the chrome's own width move. Every transition is a CSS transition, so a second ⌃⌘S mid-way turns it round from where it is.

WhatHow
Fold / unfold (⌃⌘S, the button)The edge glides 168 ↔ 50 in 220 ms, cubic-bezier(.32,.72,0,1) (the drawer curve, no bounce). Labels fade out in the first 90 ms (ease-out) and fade in during the last 120 ms (100 ms delay). Icons don't move.
Into Library / out of itThe page switches at once (selection after navigation is instant). The chrome's edge glides 168 → 290 in 220 ms on the same curve while the labels fade out, and the browser's rows fade in over the last 150 ms, 8 px from the left. On the way out it's quicker: the rows fade in 90 ms, then the edge glides back. Exit is faster than entry.
Drag the column's edge1:1 with the pointer, with no easing (direct manipulation), and the table reflows live because the user is steering it. Double-click to reset: 220 ms, drawer curve.
Details below 1240 pxThe browser column steps back behind the rail (translateX -24 px and fading, 200 ms, the drawer curve) while the inspector slides in. On close it comes back the same way (240 ms). The page lays out once.
Details inspectorOpen: the table narrows at once and the inspector slides in from the window's right edge (translateX 100 % → 0), 240 ms, drawer curve. Close: the table widens at once and the inspector slides out the same way, 200 ms, so it leaves the way it came. A selection change inside it is instant.
Tooltips (rail, rows)Fade and grow from scale(.97) at the trigger, 140 ms (--t-pop). The first waits 500 ms, the next open at once.
Favourites dragLift: 120 ms (raised, shadow, scale 1.02). The other rows part with a 200 ms ease-out transform. Drop: a spring with damping 1 and response 0.3, with no overshoot (a drop has no momentum).
Sync ring, player ringTheir arcs move linearly with progress, and nothing turns or pulses.
Reduced motionNo glides: widths change at once and the chrome's contents cross-fade in 150 ms. The inspector fades in place.

House-rule changes (for docs/hub-design-language.md, not edited now)

  1. The L frame: the sidebar has two forms, and in Library the frame holds the browser column. --sidebar-w becomes the chrome's whole width (168, 50, or 50 + the browser's width), and the foot's line and seek area follow it. Why: the frame stays one seamless material, it only changes width.
  2. One more line inside the chrome: the rail's right edge, the same hairline as the page's edge. "Only the page's edge has a line" becomes "the page's edge, and the rail's edge when the browser stands next to it". Why: without it, the icon column and the browser read as one ragged list.
  3. ListPanelFrame: Library drops the docked panel. Library gets a details inspector instead (on demand, the same widths, the same Panel inside), and its summary becomes a strip block (SummaryStrip: share · bar · two link rows, 72 px). The other pages keep the docked panel. Why: the widths table above.
  4. The sidebar's Library group retires; the sidebar shows Favourites (no counts) on pages without the browser. The two-line crate rows and the watched folders' balanced wrap retire with it: the browser's rows are one line.
  5. Motion: the "chrome moves, page lays out once" rule joins the motion table, together with the timings above.
  6. The nav's text glyphs (◉ ⟳ ✓ ≡ ◔ ⚙) become drawn icons (14 px, 1.3 px stroke, one family), the same in both forms. Why: in the rail the icon is the only label, and at 12 px ⟳ and ◉ render as dots and rings that can't be told apart. Library gets the note.
  7. Segmented: no change. With 946 / 766 px the Library strip is never tight, and "Upgrade ready" and "Lossless 290" stay whole.

Considered and not built

Details under the table (a 200 px shelf), so the table never loses a column. The spectra would get width, but the table would keep 10 rows at 1280 × 800 and only 6–8 at 1100 × 700 with the foot, which is too few to browse a crate. Page icons as a row at the top of one wide Library sidebar (Xcode's navigator bar) would save the rail's 50 px, but every icon would jump from the column to the row, against the spatial rule. The panel docked as today leaves 586 px at 1280 (see widths). A third option, a narrower inspector in place of stepping the browser back at 1100, would cut the upgrade's spectra to about 220 px, too small to see a cutoff. So the recommended structure is the user's own direction, completed: rail + browser + summary strip + the details inspector on demand. I found nothing better worth a second set of states.
