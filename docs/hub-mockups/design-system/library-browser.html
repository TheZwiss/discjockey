Library browser (sidebar rethink)

1280 × 800 · each state has a hash, e.g. #a-default

Why a rethink

The stage 5 mockup (library-sidebar.html, kept as the old design) showed a watched folder and its direct subfolders. A watched folder is often a wrapper: Rekordbox Library › Music › DJ › Hardtechno / Bounce / Rock. Its one level shows just "Music", and clicking it shows the whole library on one page, with no hint why or how to fix it. And with 35 playlists and 30 folders, one flat list needs search, favourites, folding and an order.

Decisions (both variants)

  1. Folders show their crates, not their tree. A crate is a folder that holds music files itself. Under its watched folder, each crate is one row, one indent deep, whatever its depth on disk. Wrapper folders (no music files of their own: Music, DJ, Old Sets) never get a row; the tooltip gives the full path. Why: the folders a DJ thinks in are the ones with tracks in them, and they're exactly the ones rekordbox users drag into crates; the wrapper levels are just how the disk happens to be arranged.
  2. A crate is called by its own folder name. When two crates share a name, both get their parent folder as a quiet hint: Bounce · DJ, Bounce · Old Sets. A crate inside another crate reads Hardtechno › 2019 and sorts right after its parent. Crates are A–Z under their watched folder. Why: an A–Z list is what you scan for a name; the "›" form keeps a nested crate beside its parent without making the list a tree. A crate's count is its files plus those of crates inside it (Hardtechno 180 = 142 + 2019's 38), the same rule as before (files at or below).
  3. Hiding is per crate, and only hides the row. Right-click → Hide from sidebar, or switch it off in Settings → Watched folders. The crate's files stay everywhere else: the watched folder's page, All tracks, search. Why: the sidebar is a shortcut list. Hiding never changes what's in the library.
  4. Too many crates: limit 25. A watched folder with more than 25 crates (an Artist › Album library: 312 albums) shows as one row with no chevron. Settings says "312 folders with music, too many for the sidebar: switch on the ones you want to see", and the ones you switch on show under it. Why 25: 25 rows of 30 px are 750 px, a whole sidebar at 800. A curated crate set is usually well under that, while an album library is in the hundreds, so the limit only ever catches the second kind. Search still finds every album.
  5. A watched folder's page shows everything in it. Its Subfolder column uses the crate names from the sidebar, without the wrapper levels ("Hardtechno › 2019", not "Music › DJ › Hardtechno › 2019"), and it's the page's default sort. Hidden crates are listed too.
  6. Search filters names, not tracks. The magnifier in the LIBRARY heading (or ⌘K) turns the heading into a filter field. Everything that doesn't match goes away. Folded sections open for the search. Matches keep their section and, for a crate, its watched folder row as context. The matched letters are drawn in strong text. The first match has the keyboard highlight: Enter opens it, ↑ ↓ move, Esc closes. It also finds hidden crates and the 312 albums that aren't in the sidebar. ⌘F stays the page's own track search. Why ⌘K: ⌘F already searches tracks; two meanings for one key would be a trap.
  7. Favourites are the user's own order. The section shows up only once something is pinned (right-click → Add to favourites, or drag a row in; while you drag a row, an empty Favourites slot appears so it has somewhere to land). You order it by dragging: the lifted row follows the pointer and the rows below make room. A pinned crate gets the folder outline, because it now stands outside its watched folder. A pinned row stays in its own section too. When the page is open, the pill goes on the topmost row for it.
  8. Playlists come in download priority (playlists.txt, set on the Playlists page, so no dragging here), or A–Z. The sort button in the header opens a two-item menu (Download priority ✓ · A–Z). The header counts the playlists (35, switched-off ones included).
  9. Folding. Every section header folds (the whole header row toggles it, and its chevron sits on the counts' right edge). Each watched folder folds on its own (D8's chevron, right before the count). Folding is remembered. Folding also hides the current row; the page title still says where you are, and opening a page from search or a link unfolds its section.
  10. Section headers are quieter than the LIBRARY caption. They use 12 px semibold in sentence case, against the caption's 11 px capitals, so they read as parts of the Library group, not as new groups. Changed from D4: D4 had no captions, only 6 px pauses. With 35 playlists the pause scrolled away, and a fold needs something to click.
  11. A widens the sidebar from 168 to 200 px (one token, every page). Why: the first shot of this mockup at 168 px showed "Hardtech…" for both Hardtechno and Hardtechno › 2019, and "Bounce · …" twice. A crate row starts 31 px in, so at 168 px it has about 75 px for its name, and crate names then can't be told apart. At 200 px a crate has about 115 px and a playlist about 110 px. Cost: every page is 32 px narrower (Library's table at 1280: ≈ 676 px instead of 708; it keeps every column). B doesn't need this, because its column has its own width.

What each signal means

SignalMeans exactly this, nowhere else
Row at half strengthA switched-off playlist (Playlists page): its files stay, nothing new downloads. The sidebar's only dim. Hidden crates aren't dimmed: they have no row.
Yellow ! in the icon slotThe watched folder's drive isn't connected, or the folder isn't found. The same mark as Settings → Watched folders and AwayTag. Name and count stay at full strength (the files are still in the library).
Service logo, own coloursWhich service the playlist is read from (Spotify, Qobuz, SoundCloud, YouTube Music). Unchanged.
Folder outlineA folder on disk that stands on its own: a watched folder, a pinned crate in Favourites, or a Library folder no playlist uses. A crate under its watched folder has no icon; it indents.
"· DJ" after a name, label greyAnother crate in the sidebar has the same name. This is the folder above it.
"›" inside a nameA crate inside another crate (Hardtechno › 2019).
Number at the rightPlaylist: its songs (also those with no file yet). Watched folder, crate: files in it and in the crates inside it. All tracks, Not in a playlist: files.
Number after a header's nameHow many playlists there are. Only Playlists has one; Favourites is short, and Folders has its counts on the rows.
ChevronFolds what's below: pointing down = open. On a header: the section. On a watched folder: its crates. A watched folder with no crates shown has none.
Strong letters in a nameOnly while searching: what matched.
Orange wash on one row while searchingThe keyboard highlight: Enter opens it, ↑ ↓ move it. The grey pill stays the page you are on.
Orange ring round a rowThe row the open context menu acts on (orange = action).

A or B

A · sections in the sidebar (recommended)B · a browser column in the Library page
ReachEvery playlist and crate is one click away from any page (from To-do or Sync straight to a crate), and it's where the stage 5 user already looks.Library first, then the column: two clicks from other pages. The app sidebar stays short and calm: only the pages.
WidthThe sidebar grows to 200 px (decision 11), so every page loses 32 px. Library's table is still ≈ 676 px at 1280 and keeps Track, Artist, Subfolder, Playlist and Quality. Names get ≈ 110–115 px; the longest playlist names still end in "…" (tooltip).The column is 216 px: names get ≈ 130 px. At 1280 the table falls to ≈ 480 px, so Artist and Playlist columns go, and the right panel takes 360 px of what's left. At 1100 it's ≈ 400 px: Track, Quality and little else. To fix that, the panel would have to fold away until a track is chosen.
SearchBehind the magnifier (one click or ⌘K).Always visible. But it's a second search field on the same page as the track search, and they're easy to mix up.
ScaleBoth scroll the same list. A's list scrolls in the app's chrome, so the pages (Dashboard … Settings) stay fixed above and below it.Same list, with more width per name and the full page height.

Recommendation: A. The width B costs at the window sizes the hub actually runs at is worse than the cut names in A, and reaching a crate from anywhere is the point of the sidebar. B is the right choice only if the right panel goes away (or turns into a sheet).
